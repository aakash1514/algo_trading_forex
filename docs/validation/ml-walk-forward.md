# ML walk-forward evaluation (step 4)

Generated by `python -m ml_service.report` on 2026-10-12 06:53 UTC at commit `802e20e`. Historical walk-forward
test on the imported Dukascopy history, **not live signals**.

## Setup

- **Target:** direction of the next H1 bar, mid open to close (exact in integer points); flat bars,
  bars under 30 minutes and the last bar have no target.
- **Features (10, bars <= t only):** close-to-close, own open-to-close and gap log returns, log range,
  spread in pips, log(minutes/60), sin/cos of hour and weekday. Scaled with training-period statistics.
- **Walk-forward:** per pair, 7 expanding folds; test year Y, validation Y-1, training 2010 to Y-2. A sample
  belongs to the period of its target bar. Validation is used only for early stopping and model choice.
- **Models:** LSTM grid lstm-h32-w32, lstm-h64-w32 (chosen per fold on validation log loss);
  logistic baseline logistic-w32. Training: Adam 1e-3, batch 512, <= 40 epochs,
  early stopping on validation log loss (patience 5), fixed seed, deterministic algorithms, 8 threads.
- **Grid decision (validation only):** window 96 was dropped after the timed round (EUR/USD, test 2022):
  2.7x slower and no better on validation log loss (h32: 0.690491 at window 96 vs 0.690480 at window 32).
- **Baselines (same samples):** logistic regression on the same window; majority direction of the training
  targets; persistence (next bar moves like bar t); anti-persistence (the opposite).

## Rule (fixed before training)

The LSTM beats a baseline only if its pooled 2016-2022 accuracy is higher and the pooled McNemar test
(exact binomial on the discordant pairs) gives p < 0.05 after a Holm correction across all 16 pooled
LSTM-vs-baseline tests (4 pairs x 4 baselines). Raw and adjusted p-values are both shown.

## Conclusion

Under the fixed rule the LSTM beats 9 of 16 pair-baseline comparisons: 
EUR/USD vs Logistic, EUR/USD vs Majority, EUR/USD vs Persistence, GBP/USD vs Logistic, GBP/USD vs Majority, GBP/USD vs Persistence, USD/JPY vs Persistence, XAU/USD vs Majority, XAU/USD vs Persistence.
It does not beat anti-persistence for any pair.
The pooled LSTM accuracy is below the break-even accuracy (next paragraph) for 4 of 4 pairs (all of them): it would not cover the spread.

Accuracy needed just to cover the spread: trading each predicted direction from the target bar's open to
its close pays about one spread, so with equal average win and loss sizes the break-even accuracy is
0.5 + spread / (2 x average move): EUR/USD 53.24% (spread 0.46 pips, move 7.15 pips); GBP/USD 56.44% (spread 1.33 pips, move 10.34 pips); USD/JPY 53.67% (spread 0.57 pips, move 7.81 pips); XAU/USD 60.18% (spread 36.39 pips, move 178.76 pips).

### Original and corrected criterion, side by side

Corrected criterion (decided 2026-10-12, docs/OVERNIGHT-QUESTIONS.md #3; evaluated on the same four control
runs, no retraining): the control's balanced accuracy 95% interval must include 0.5. The confusion counts
are recovered exactly from each run's stored accuracy, balanced accuracy and test up-count; the interval is
the normal approximation, 0.5 x sqrt(TPR(1-TPR)/P + TNR(1-TNR)/N) x 1.96 around the estimate.

| Pair | Original: must not beat majority | Train / test up-rate | TP of P / TN of N | Balanced accuracy [95% interval] | Corrected: interval includes 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR/USD | passed | 50.28% / 48.95% | 2149 of 3043 / 867 of 3173 | 0.4897 [0.4785, 0.5009] | passed |
| GBP/USD | **FAILED** (beats it) | 50.10% / 48.84% | 2387 of 3039 / 742 of 3183 | 0.5093 [0.4989, 0.5196] | passed |
| USD/JPY | passed | 50.82% / 52.44% | 3128 of 3261 / 119 of 2957 | 0.4997 [0.4948, 0.5046] | passed |
| XAU/USD | passed | 50.61% / 49.55% | 1735 of 2926 / 1168 of 2979 | 0.4925 [0.4800, 0.5050] | passed |

Why the original criterion was mis-specified: a control trained on shuffled labels has no signal, so it
should predict at chance, and the right reference is chance (balanced accuracy 0.5), not the majority
baseline. The majority baseline predicts the training years' more common direction; when the test year's
balance flips, it scores below 50%. GBP/USD's training years were 50.10% up, so it predicted up, but 2022
was 48.84% up: the baseline scored 48.84%, and a chance-level control (50.29%, balanced accuracy 0.5093)
beat it. That is not a leak. The original result stays reported above; the corrected criterion passes for
all four pairs.
